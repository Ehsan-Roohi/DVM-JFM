Manuscript Review: JFM-2026-1439 
E. Roohi, “Closure-channel identiﬁability and two-channel recovery in monatomic kinetic normal 
shocks,” Journal of Fluid Mechanics, 2026. 
Summary and Recommendation 
The author investigates the identiﬁability of fourth-order closure information associated with the R26 
moment hierarchy in monatomic normal shocks. Speciﬁcally, the author shows that the fourth-order 
information in the heat-ﬂux budget depends a on combined channel based on the tensorial closure 
moment and the scalar-excess. The author identiﬁes the null space of this mapping, which indicates 
that agreement with the heat-ﬂux budget does not guarantee accurate recovery of the individual 
fourth-order closure variables. 
With this context in mind, the author uses a series of discrete-velocity-method (DVM) reference 
solutions along with a two-budget reconstruction technique to recover the tensorial closure moment 
from the combined channel and a scalar-excess budget. The manuscript then examines a hierarchy 
of kinetic collision models and DSMC diagnostics to distinguish the collision-independent 
observation channel from the model-dependent production of the scalar-excess component. This 
evaluation framework may provide a useful way of assessing whether kinetic closures, moment 
models, and reduced-order solvers recover the higher-order information they are intended to 
represent. 
While I found this work intriguing, I have a number of questions regarding the methodology, the 
deﬁnition and presentation of several quantities, and the level of detail provided for readers who are 
not specialists in kinetic theory or high-order moment methods. I also found the overall organization 
of the manuscript difﬁcult to follow in several places. As such, I recommend major revision before 
the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
General Comments 
*For the general ﬂuid-mechanics reader, I would highly recommend a adding table or brief outline 
in section 2 that summarizes the principal moments used in the analysis, their mathematical 
deﬁnitions, their physical interpretations, and perhaps their role in the moment hierarchy. As 
written, a considerable baseline knowledge is assumed, including familiarity with the  R26 
formulation, the meaning of the  subscript, scalar fourth-order excess, active zones, source 
neutrality, ES-FP drift/diffusion parameters, etc. Providing a slightly more background would make 
the manuscript much more accessible. 
*The coupling of the tensorial fourth-order moment and scalar fourth-order moment in the heat-
ﬂux equation appears to be part of the established R26 formulation. I did not think this historical 
context was sufﬁciently clear in the manuscript. The author should explain more explicitly which 
mathematical structure is already established and which aspect constitutes the present 
contribution. As I understand the manuscript, the important new element is not simply the 
appearance of both moments in the heat-ﬂux equation, but the interpretation of that relationship 
as an identiﬁability/observation map, identiﬁcation of its null space, and the subsequent 
framework for assessing kinetic models. Clarifying this distinction would help establish the novelty 
and signiﬁcance of the work.

*The manuscript should more clearly distinguish between full kinetic-model shock calculations and

methodologies appear to be missing from the manuscript. For example, the manuscript describes 
the removal of the plateau offset from both  and , but this procedure is not included and a 
historical reference is not provided. These details are relevant for a general audience.
