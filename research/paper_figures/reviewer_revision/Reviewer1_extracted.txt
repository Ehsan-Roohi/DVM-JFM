1 General Evaluation and Significance
The submission addresses a foundational and highly contemporary bottleneck in computational
fluid dynamics and kinetic theory: the mathematical unidentifiability and physical unfaithful-
ness of high-order moment closures, particularly those driven by data-driven frameworks or
machine learning (ML) optimization strategies.
Instead of treating the moment closure problem as a standard statistical curve-fitting or
regression exercise, the author frames it as a rigorous geometric observability problem. I
find core premise to be both elegant and in some sense alarming. In a steady, one-dimensional
monatomic normal shock, the classical spatial transport operator acts as an observational sieve.
That is, it compresses the independent high-order variables—specifically, the trace-free tensorial
anisotropy (Rcl
xx) and the isotropic scalar-excess of the high-speed molecular tails (∆)—into a
single, combined kinematic transport channel ( S = Rcl
xx + 1
3∆). Now, this linear observation
operator possesses a non-trivial one-dimensional null space. Unconstrained optimization mod-
els (such as Physics-Informed Neural Networks) can therefore end up achieving absolute zero
macroscopic residuals on fluid profiles while predicting entirely unphysical, distorted phase-space
distribution tails! The proposed ’Two-Channel Recovery Framework’ resolves this degeneracy
by introducing an independent, collision-dependent scalar-excess budget derived directly from
discrete velocity methods (DVM) and stochastically verified via direct simulation Monte Carlo
(DSMC) binary collision logging.
This work is original, timely, and has broad implications for the stabilization of AI-driven
fluid solvers. However, a few physical, mathematical, and algorithmic assumptions must be
clarified and supported before this manuscript can be accepted for publication in a premier
journal.
2 Major Comments (Scientific and Mathematical Rigour)
2.1 Structural Stability, Realizability, and the Hyperbolic-Elliptic Transition
The manuscript very well highlights how data-driven models ”cheat” the macroscopic transport
equations by introducing localized spatial hallucinations ( η(x)) along the null space axis vector
n = [−1, 3]T . However, I find that the physical consequences would extend far beyond a ”poorly
determined tail structure.”
1
When an unconstrained optimizer over-inflates the high-velocity scalar tail (∆+3η) and com-
presses the longitudinal direction (Rcl
xx − η), it violates the underlying realizability boundary
of the Hermite polynomial expansion. This makes the implied probability distribution function
dip into negative values. This crossing has implications for stability considerations. The closed
26-moment flux Jacobian matrix which figures therein suffers a change in its spectrum. (Eigen-
values instantly change from from real numbers into complex conjugate pairs) The differential
equations system ceases to be hyperbolic/parabolic and becomes an ill-posed elliptic-temporal
equation. One then needs to worry about having triggered the infamous Bobylev instability
(exponential error amplification at high spatial frequencies).
Suggestion: The author should explicitly comment on connection of the data-driven ’iden-
tifiability failure.’ to this shift in the nature of the Jacobian matrix spectrum. It would be
ideal if one could prove that enforcing the secondary collisional budget stabilizes the principal
symbol of the PDE by keeping its eigenvalues strictly real, thereby mathematically restoring
H¨ ormander’s hypoelliptic structure. Bringing such into the discussion in the submission would
better guide the reader.
2.2 Dimensional Coupling Limits and Multi-Dimensional Expansion
The algebraic identity that enables zero-tensor-data recovery ( Rcl
xx = S − 1
3∆) relies funda-
mentally on the decoupled, idealized symmetries of a 1-D normal shock. In this specific 1-D
setting, only the longitudinal element of the fourth-rank composite tensor enters the stream-
wise heat-flux budget. The observation operator thus turns out to be into a simple row matrix
H = [1, 1/3].
In any realistic 3-D configuration or curvilinear aerodynamic mesh (e.g., like in case of a
hypersonic flow over a blunt body), the observable maps to the full spatial divergence of the
high-order moment tensors. In such a situation, the null space would change from a simple 1-D
line into a high-dimensional space. This brings in more difficulty.
Suggestion: The author must include a dedicated discussion detailing the boundaries of this
1-D geometric decoupling. The manuscript needs to clarify whether a two-scalar budget system
remains mathematically sufficient to prevent symbol degeneracy in a fully multi-dimensional
configuration. Maybe as future work, an approach could be taken using multivariate tensor-loss
matrices clustered via rotation algebras ( so(3))
2.3 Non-Linear Collision Dynamics and Molecular Potential Dependencies
The author shows that the Shakhov collision target modification remains completely source-
neutral with respect to the scalar-excess budget. Because the Shakhov correction relies on
an odd-power velocity polynomial, its integration against the strictly even-power weight |c|4
vanishes identically to zero over a symmetric velocity domain. This is important to note.
However, core molecular interactions do not obey clean polynomial relaxation models (like BGK
or Shakhov). In the true Boltzmann limit or inside realistic particle potentials (such as Hard
Spheres), the collision frequency depends heavily on the relative velocities of the colliding pairs
(|v − v∗|). Fast-moving tail particles collide at significantly higher rates, introducing non-linear
structural couplings that do not relax uniformly.
Suggestion: The author should verify whether the reported near-perfect algebraic recovery
under the Shakhov model degrades when exposed to the highly non-linear, asymmetric tail-
cascade physics of a true Boltzmann operator. The manuscript should demonstrate that the
2.4–4.1% error envelope remains robust against varying molecular collision cross-sections.

2.4 Thermodynamic Consistency and H-Theorem Guardrails for Anisotropic
Source Terms
The author provides an elegant demonstration that both the ES-BGK and ES-FP models align
structurally with the scalar-excess budget. I find however, that the physical anomaly in Table
1 requires critical attention. The data reveals that the anisotropic velocity diffusion operator
within the ES-FP target correction yields an amplitude effect that is exactly five times larger and
carries the opposite mathematical sign relative to the ES-BGK correction. Would this negative
sign configuration violate Boltzmann’s H-theorem under extreme, highly localized hypersonic
non-equilibrium profiles? If the ES-FP diffusion operator accidentally functions as a localized
energy pump, say, at specific high-speed tail frequencies, then one needs to worry about the
second law of thermodynamics.
Suggestion: The manuscript should include a formal mathematical audit of the local entropy
production rate σS ≥ 0) across the Mach 5 shock core.
2.5 Operational Sensitivity to High-Order Numerical Boundary Conditions
The proposed two-channel framework successfully stabilizes and uniquely identifies the interior
moment tracks of the R26 framework. The practical deployment of this closure remains highly
academic though, without a clear definition of boundary infrastructure. High-order PDE mo-
ment closures are notoriously hyper-sensitive to boundary layer artifacts, numerical reflections,
and slip-wall anomalies.
Suggestion: The author should explicitly define the structural translation rules for map-
ping these recovered interior fourth-order closures ( Rcl
xx, ∆) onto solid wall grids, inlets, and
free-stream outlets. These regularized closures may be able to ingest standard hydrodynamic
boundary constraints without triggering local spectrum mutations or numerical edge blow-ups,
the hypoelliptic stabilization.This meeds to be commented upon. Alternatively, the framework
remains restricted to idealized, infinite-domain shocks, which if desired by the author, should
be commented upon in the manuscript.
2.6 Phase-Space Coupling with Polyatomic Internal Energy Degrees of Free-
dom
The mathematical derivation of the two-channel framework is strictly constrained to monatomic
gases where translation dominates the fourth-order scalar-excess. Applications like say, hyper-
sonic re-entry configurations would involve polyatomic gases with active rotational and vi-
brational modes. These internal energy states alter the structure of the Boltzmann collision
operator through cross-mode relaxation channels.
Suggestion: The author should provide a theoretical perspective on how the identifiability
of the scalar budget changes when translational energy balances couple directly with internal
energy distributions. The manuscript needs to clarify whether this structural decoupling retains
its one-dimensional null-space characteristics under polyatomic conditions.
2.7 Asymptotic Hydrodynamic Limits and Continuum Near-Equilibrium Scal-
ing
A vital requirement for any high-order moment framework is its asymptotic reduction to Navier-
Stokes-Fourier equations via Chapman-Enskog expansion as the Knudsen number approaches
zero. The manuscript demonstrates excellent performance inside the highly non-equilibrium
shock core, but it lacks verification in the near-equilibrium limit.
Suggestion: The author should show that the machine-learning-driven closure preserves cor-
rect scaling transitions without introducing artificial numerical regularization terms. The
3
learned closure operator may distort classical Prandtl number or bulk viscosity definitions dur-
ing scale contractions.
3 Numerical and Simulation Validation Comments
3.1 DVM High-Energy Tail Truncation Controls
The deterministic ground-truth baseline relies on Discrete Velocity Method (DVM) tensor-
product quadrature grids ranging up to 141 × 27 × 27 points for Mach 5 shocks. Because the
computation of the scalar-excess (∆ =
R
|c|4(f − Md) dv) applies a fourth-power weight to the
velocity coordinates, even minute sampling or boundary truncation errors in the far fringes of
velocity space are exponentially amplified.
Suggestion: The exact velocity-space boundary controls ( vmax) which were used for the DVM
quadratures would help here. Providing them can help show that the reported curves are
entirely free from unphysical grid clipping or artificial plateau-corrected truncation artifacts in
the highly non-equilibrium zone.
(Furthermore, inside a strong hypersonic shock layer, the molecular velocity distribution
splits into bimodal populations representing cold upstream beams and hot downstream states.
Tensor-product velocity grids if they lack adaptive or multi-scale mesh layouts, can misrepresent
this structural bifurcation. The author should explicitly comment upon how this bimodal
distribution profile interacts with the fixed DVM grid alignment. Phase space mesh limits may
be causing localized numerical integration artifactsin training data for the fourth order tail
metrics)
3.2 Statistical Variance Filters in DSMC Particle Logging
In Section III, the paper uses standalone Direct Simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) particle
simulations to log the change in energy ( δ∆) during accepted binary collision events. Extreme
high-speed particle states in individual cell volumes ( Vj) would be rare and averaging windows
need to be carefully considered. Fourth-order moments are inherently sensitive to stochastic
noise. Yet the reported collisional source curve ( QDSMC
∆ ) is of a smooth nature. This is highly
impressive.
Suggestion: Provide a rigorous breakdown of the sample size independence. The author
should show that the reported 0.987 correlation with the local density gradient is a genuine
physical localization of the collision-production channel. Providing details of sample size in-
dependence would rule out smoothing artifacts stemming from the temporal or cell-volume
ensemble-averaging window (∆tsamp).
(Beyond sample size tracking, the author should address potential subgrid numerical cor-
relations stemming from low particle-per-cell (PPC) counts. When tracking a fourth-power
velocity weightacross individual cells, a low PPC count can induce a non-random statistical
bias. This happens due to the artificial local depletion of high-speed molecular collision pairs
inside the stochastic simulator. It would be better if the author provides a standalone PPC
sensitivity audit for the logged collision source term curve. This would clearly demonstrate
that the smooth behaviour of the profile reflects true physical production rather than subgrid
particle grouping effects)
3.3 Resolution Degeneracy and Spatial Sparsity within the Hypersonic Ac-
tive Core
In Section IV, the manuscript demonstrates that sampling the continuous ∆ tail field at only 24
uniform points yields a reconstruction error below 4.5%. This sparse-probe utility is accepted too
passively in the current text. The active non-equilibrium core of a Mach 5 shock discontinuity is
4
mathematically narrow—spanning a spatial envelope of only a few mean free paths. A uniform
24-point distribution across the entire computational domain means that only 2 or 3 sampling
probes actually land within the zone of violent spatial gradients. This poses an important
difficulty.
Suggestion: The author must challenge the robustness of this interpolation by evaluating how
the recovery error scales under acute, core-localized sparsity. A way further is by non-uniform
grid sensitivity analysis. When the core features sudden geometric gaps in data availability,
that could be used to prove that the radial-basis interpolant does not introduce spurious high-
frequency oscillations.
4 Clarity, Notation, and Formatting Comments
• Symbol Unification: Throughout the fluid budget derivations, the non-dimensionalization
symbols alternate slightly between general moving-frame relaxation scales (τ) and explicit
macroscopic Knudsen scales (Kn). These should be unified to preserve clarity for readers
tracking multi-scale asymptotic expansions.
• Table 1 Clarification: In Table 1, the manuscript establishes an interestingly strict
5:1 variance ratio between the Ellipsoidal Statistical Fokker-Planck (ES-FP) and Ellip-
soidal Statistical BGK (ES-BGK) anisotropic diffusion targets. The accompanying caption
should include a concise physical explanation as to why the Fokker-Planck drift/diffusion
mapping scales exactly five times larger in magnitude and opposite in sign for a correct-
Prandtl calibration ( β = −5/4).
• Figure Labeling: In Figure 7’s multi-panel layout, please ensure that the spatial deriva-
tive curves and the stochastically logged binary collision counts are plotted using identical,
explicitly normalized coordinate axes ( x/λ1) Such would allow for immediate visual audit
of the closed scalar budget.
5 Recommendation
Major Revision. The core concept of this manuscript is very strong and introduces a vital
mathematical gauge for validating machine learning architectures in non-equilibrium thermody-
namics. The author should address mainly three points as detailed above, the multi-dimensional
null-space scaling limitations, the mathematical hyperbolicity limits of the closed symbol, and
the robustness against non-linear molecular potentials.
